# Fresh tactical benchmark: consequences before strategic weights

All **12 fresh positions completed**, producing **3,696 matched root samples**
and one additional completed-turn continuation per sample. Three positions
contain alternatives with scenario-wise better terminal outcomes under those
continuations. None establishes that a new strategic weighting is correct.
No production code, search weights, admission rules or player behavior changed.
The previous full-game result remains 16/24; this experiment adds no games to it.

## What was frozen

Six new six-Pokemon lineup pairs, each tested at the opening and after two
completed turns generated by frozen V2 on both sides. These cover intended
questions about concentrated damage, spread/trades, speed control, redirection,
switching and competing attackers. A category names the design question; it
does not guarantee that the resulting position contains a favorable setup,
trade or winning route. All twelve were retained without result-based filtering.

The roster pairs, actual team text, preview 1234, seeds, fixed warmup policy,
probe-selection rules, four RNG labels, measurement definitions, limitations
and source hashes were frozen in `fresh-tactical-manifest-v2-2026-10-15.json`
before generating outcomes. An initial preflight rejected a duplicate Life Orb
in a six-member team. Armarouge was assigned Charcoal consistently, and the
inputs were re-frozen before any tactical sample. The rejected manifest remains
in ignored local runs; no rejected result was removed from the successful set.

These are fresh arrangements and positions, with familiar species and mostly
familiar sets. They are now development data, not a reusable blind holdout.

## Controlled experiment

- Evaluate the existing one-turn 8/3 native search in one explicit hypothetical
  world. This isolates consequence evaluation from public admission and hidden
  state reconstruction; it is not a public-only full-game player trial.
- Compare all eight root candidates plus deterministic legal witnesses for
  shielding, switching, speed control, disruption, targeting, spread and Mega
  commands. There are at most 14 distinct root candidates per position.
- Use the original three native replies plus deterministic frozen-V2/category
  witnesses, up to six distinct replies. Every candidate faces the same reply
  list and four coupled RNG labels, including the production control label.
- Complete replacement/pivot phases and the next turn with frozen V2 using
  each side's own public observation. The phase limit is six. This continuation
  can miss better native tactics and is not an optimal-play oracle.
- Measure native terminal facts, surviving teams and named direct-damage
  actors. No new scalar score, immutable finisher or move bonus is introduced.

Each first/second-turn count is conditional on the supplied exact state,
response family, phase decisions, continuation policy and RNG labels. Counts
are scenarios, not estimated win probabilities. Re-seeding at hypothetical
branches is deliberate coupling, not a model of full-game RNG probabilities.

## Findings requiring further review

| Fresh position | Search choice | Alternative | Terminal outcomes after continuation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spread/trade, turn 3 | Earthquake + Mega Gardevoir Protect | Earthquake + Mega Gardevoir Expanding Force | 9/24 wins versus 13/24; both 0 losses |
| Speed-control lineage, turn 3 | Rillaboom Wood Hammer + Metagross Psychic Fangs | Rillaboom Protect + Metagross Steel Roller | Both 0/24 wins; losses fall from 8/24 to 0/24 |
| Alternative attackers, turn 1 | Mega Gardevoir Mystical Fire + Metagross Protect | Mega Gardevoir Hyper Voice + Metagross Steel Roller | Alternative adds 2 terminal wins; no added terminal losses |

The second row is a survival witness, not a demonstrated winning route. Its
remaining positions can still lose later. First-turn survivors and direct
damage counts alone do not establish eventual value.

The spread/trade offensive alternatives were already in the native shortlist.
Their worst immediate utilities are about **994.21**, versus **1071.66** for
Protect. Default-RNG utilities for both lines match the original native leaf
scores against all three original responses, including phase completion.
However, the four additional finishes comprise one extra-RNG scenario against
an original response and three scenarios against an added switching response.
There is **no terminal-win difference in the original three/default-RNG cells**.
It would be premature to attribute this result solely to material preservation.

The survival alternative in the second row is also already in the shortlist.
Its immediate worst utility is about **-3242.86**, versus **-2147.73** for the
selected attack. Native control scores match for both lines. Avoided losses
occur against two original responses across all four RNG labels, including
the original label. This is a stronger short-continuation counterexample for
review, but frozen V2 might be mishandling the position after the selected
attack. It also demonstrates why a general Protect penalty would be mistaken.

The alternative-attacker difference appears only against an added switching
response, and the frozen-V2 phase-completed leaf scores do not all match the
native kernel's own phase choices. It is exploratory evidence of a possible
trade/conversion route, not a clean isolated evaluator failure. Seven of its
24 first-turn samples have fewer surviving allies than the selected line.

Across the suite, **zero positions** contain a scenario-wise first-turn terminal
improvement witness under this definition. That does not establish good KO
selection, favorable material trades or optimality in nonterminal positions.
Native search disagrees with frozen V2 in **11/12** roots. Disagreement alone
does not show better decisions. All optimal-action labels remain unproven.

## Validation and evidence

All 24 team entries across twelve cases passed native format validation. Every
probe command was checked against the native legal menu. The audit checks all
3,696 action/reply/RNG entries for complete, identical coupled coverage and no
duplicates; every native terminal win/loss retains its existing +/-1,000,000
utility. It checks manifest/source hashes and all twelve case identities.

**150 direct completed-turn control cells** reproduce the original native
utilities exactly. Other cells pause for replacement/pivot decisions; those
use frozen V2 for probe completion and are not silently claimed to reproduce
the kernel's continuation selector. The report separately checks score parity
for selected/alternative pairs. Ruff and Python syntax checks passed for both
new scripts. No production tests were changed, and no new strength claim is made.

Tracked evidence:

- `fresh-tactical-manifest-v2-2026-10-15.json`: frozen inputs and protocol.
- `fresh-tactical-results-2026-10-15.json`: all action measurements.
- `fresh-tactical-audit-2026-10-15.json`: coupled-grid audit and conditional
  terminal witnesses, including original-response/default-RNG comparisons.

Raw matrices, root public views and branch measurements remain local at
`runs/fresh-tactical/v1/results.json.gz`, excluded from Git.

With `PYTHONPATH=src;scripts` and the project virtualenv:

```text
python scripts/fresh_tactical_benchmark.py evaluate --output runs/fresh-tactical/NEW-RUN --report reviews/NEW-RESULTS.json
python scripts/fresh_tactical_report.py --archive runs/fresh-tactical/NEW-RUN/results.json.gz --manifest reviews/fresh-tactical-manifest-v2-2026-10-15.json --output reviews/NEW-AUDIT.json
```

The next experiment should replace the frozen-V2 follow-up with matched native
follow-up decisions in these three retained cases, keeping roots and opponent
responses fixed. That tests whether the apparent misses belong to leaf utility,
response/RNG coverage, phase selection or continuation behavior before changing
strategic weights. No blanket preference for attacking or preserving survives
this benchmark. Competing winning routes remain a question to test, not labels
to insert into the scorer.
